  - Abstract (twice — main text, states "n=400, all directions")

### Comment 2 — Selection bias from corpus filtering, undiscussed
- **Table 16** shows the uncurated C++ ablation set has a 23.25% zero-CES failure rate (93/400); the curated cross-language corpus has 0.25% (1/400).
- Section V-A describes the curated corpus as built by *"selecting compilable and interpretable programs with entry point identification"* — i.e., filtering criteria that plausibly remove exactly the hard cases the CES extractor struggles with.
- Threat Group 2 ("Coverage and Extraction Scope") discusses *construct validity* limitations of the taxonomy, but never names **selection bias** as a distinct threat, and never discusses that the curated 400-corpus itself may be a non-random, easier subsample of the full submission pool.

### Comment 3 — No single-view ablation
- **Table 8** shows the in-sample C++ optimum weights are (0.00, 0.05, 0.95) — essentially all-CES, almost-zero WL. This is presented without commentary on what it implies about the WL (structural) view's contribution.

### Comment 4 — 64% adversarial floor mixes incompatible n

## Step 2: Math formatting pass

**Checklist item:** *"Ensure all math equations are properly formatted according to IEEE guidelines."*
